Some of the oldest Greek inscriptions were written boustrophedon (literally 'as the ox turns') where the direction of the text's flow and the direction the letters face would switch at the end of each line, as an ox plowing a field would reverse directions. The earliest known alphabetic inscriptions date to around 2000 BC in the Ugaritic script, a cuneiform abjad used in what is now Syria. The Phoenician alphabet, a major ancestor of modern alphabets, was developed around 1050 BC. It was an abjad consisting of 22 consonant letters and no vowels. The Greek alphabet was developed from the Phoenician abjad and was the first alphabet to have distinct letters for vowel sounds. The Latin alphabet was derived from the Greek alphabet and is the most widely used writing system in the world today.
